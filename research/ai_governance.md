# AI governance and regulations in automotive software

## Scope

Emerging AI governance, regional regulations, AI Act, functional safety/cybersecurity of AI, data confidentiality, IP protection, certification challenges.

## Key themes

- EU AI Act: high-risk AI systems in safety-critical automotive contexts require risk management, data governance, transparency, human oversight, and conformity assessment.
- Functional safety (ISO 26262) and SOTIF (ISO 21448) must be extended for AI/ML components; traditional V-model assumptions break for learned behavior.
- Cybersecurity (ISO/SAE 21434, UNECE R155) applies to AI pipelines, data flows, and model updates.
- Data confidentiality and IP protection: training data, model weights, and telemetry are sensitive; cross-border data transfer constraints.
- Certification challenges: lack of standards for AI component certification, explainability, and reproducibility.
- Human oversight: AI cannot be solely responsible for safety-critical decisions; human-in-the-loop required.

## Problem interlinks

- W5-012..W5-025 (AI adoption barriers)
- W3-033 (ML testing), W3-034 (scenario coverage)
- W4-029 (AI tool trust/certification)

## Evidence limitations

- Regulatory landscape evolving; dates and requirements subject to change.
- No automotive-specific AI certification standard fully established as of 2026.
